
Thank you for the follow-up. We agree that the original submission did not directly measure the Appendix-D geometry and that noiseless results alone cannot establish robustness. In response, we began additional multi-seed geometry and noisy finite-shot experiments. These required independently trained target checkpoints and repeated circuit simulations across shot counts, simulator seeds, and noise conditions, so they took longer than a standard ablation. The analyses are now complete. [for detailed discussion and tables see https://github.com/CartwheelX/TQE-qurift/blob/main/docs/Reviewer%201myw.md]

**Backend-derived noise and finite shots.** We evaluated five representative structural configurations varying feature-map family, encoder repetitions, and variational depth. Their exact-inference loss-MIA AUCs ranged from 0.521 to 0.700. Each configuration was trained with three target-model seeds, giving 15 checkpoints. We evaluated the same member and non-member records using exact inference, ideal finite-shot Aer, and Aer with a noisy beckend `ibm_kingston`. Shot-based runs used 128, 512, and 1,024 shots with ten seeds.

Finite-shot uncertainty and backend-derived noise reduced membership leakage, but did not remove the association between structural configuration and mean attack AUC in this subset. Configurations with weaker leakage under exact inference continued to show weaker mean leakage, while those with stronger leakage continued to show stronger mean leakage. The AUC difference between ZZ with reps=5, depth=6 and EfficientSU2 with reps=1, depth=2 decreased from 0.179±0.030 under exact inference to 0.096±0.012 at 128 noisy shots, then increased to 0.124±0.026 and 0.132±0.036 at 512 and 1,024 shots.

Individual simulator runs sometimes reordered configurations with similar AUCs, especially at 128 shots. Mean Spearman correlation with the exact five-configuration ranking was 0.820±0.155 at 128 noisy shots, 0.920±0.092 at 512 shots, and 0.960±0.052 at 1,024 shots. Noise therefore partially mitigates leakage and introduces local variability, but the structural dependence remains detectable. This provides targeted support for our main claim that encoder design and repetition condition downstream membership risk. We will report the calibration timestamp, confirm that no ideal fallback was accepted.

AUC differences are mean±sample SD across three independently trained target seeds after averaging repetitions within each checkpoint. Spearman values are mean±sample SD across ten simulator seeds.

**Direct post-encoder geometry.** We now compute the pure-state fidelity kernel immediately after the fixed encoder and before the variational circuit. For paired reps=5 minus reps=1 contrasts, the class-similarity gap changed by -0.124±0.061, with a 95% hierarchical-bootstrap CI of [-0.158,-0.079]; kernel-label alignment changed by -0.208±0.132, CI [-0.285,-0.109]; and effective rank increased by 49.747±37.448, CI [15.791,83.870]. These new observations show that increasing repetition therefore changes the representation toward a higher-rank but less class-aligned geometry.

We agree with the reviewer’s causal distinction. Our claim is not that geometry bypasses overfitting or directly reveals membership. The evidence supports an encoder design → post-encoder geometry → downstream generalization asymmetry → membership signal pathway. The kernel analysis supports the first association, while the factorial results support repetition → gap and repetition → loss-AUC associations. Gap strongly tracks loss-AUC, with hierarchical-bootstrap Spearman ρ=0.931, CI [0.710,0.974]. These results do not causally identify geometry → gap independently of encoder choice. we will adde these results and the corresponding tables in the revised manuscript.
